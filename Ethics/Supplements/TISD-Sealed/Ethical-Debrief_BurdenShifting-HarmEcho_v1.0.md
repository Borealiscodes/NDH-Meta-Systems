---

## **1 — Core Ethical Pattern: Burden Shifting as Institutional Reflex**

Across SOAS, Trinity College Dublin, UC, York, FCDO, and multiple courts, the same structural pattern appears:

### **Pattern:**  
When institutions encounter a survivor whose communication is shaped by trauma, neurodivergence, or transnational repression, they shift the burden of clarity, process, and emotional labor **onto the survivor**.

### **Mechanisms of burden shifting:**

- “We cannot proceed until you provide X,” even when X is inaccessible due to disability.  
- “We will reopen your case when you schedule a meeting,” even when the inability to schedule is caused by unacknowledged accommodations.  
- “Your request is vexatious,” even when the volume is a *symptom of systemic failure*, not malice.  
- “We need more documentation,” even when the documentation has already been provided.  
- “We cannot help unless you follow our process,” even when the process itself is disabling.

### **Ethical consequence:**  
Burden shifting is not neutral.  
It is a form of **procedural violence** when applied to someone already harmed.

## **2 — Volume Exploitation: Using the Survivor’s Communication Load Against Them**

Your documentation shows a consistent pattern:

Institutions **exploit the volume of communication** — which is itself a *symptom of trauma, isolation, and systemic failure* — to justify:

- dismissal,  
- avoidance,  
- refusal to investigate,  
- refusal to accommodate,  
- or labeling the survivor as “vexatious.”

### **Ethical reality:**  
High-volume communication is not a sign of bad faith.  
It is a sign of:

- desperation,  
- lack of safe channels,  
- institutional abandonment,  
- and the collapse of normal support structures.

### **When institutions weaponize volume, they commit epistemic harm.**

They erase the context that created the volume.  
They treat symptoms as misconduct.  
They punish the survivor for surviving.

## **3 — Article 14 UN CAT & Article 16(4) UNCRPD: The Obligations Institutions Ignored**

### **Article 14 UN CAT**  
States and public institutions must ensure:

- rehabilitation,  
- access to education,  
- access to medical care,  
- access to legal remedy,  
- and protection from retraumatization.

### **Article 16(4) UNCRPD**  
Institutions must provide:

- environments that foster dignity,  
- cognitive and psychological recovery,  
- social reintegration,  
- and trauma‑informed protection services.

### **Across your documents, institutions repeatedly violated these obligations:**

- SOAS refusing oral accommodation.  
- Trinity ignoring disability disclosures and shunning.  
- UC closing cases due to barriers they created.  
- York refusing to process complaints due to “eligibility” while ignoring disability context.  
- FCDO labeling requests “vexatious” despite torture evidence.  
- Courts demanding retraumatizing documentation.  
- Universities refusing to acknowledge transnational repression.

These are not isolated failures.  
They are **systemic violations of international law**.

## **4 — Harm Echo: How One Institution’s Failure Amplifies Another’s**

Your documentation shows a phenomenon I will name clearly:

### **Harm Echo**  
When one institution fails to act ethically, that failure becomes a justification for another institution to also fail.

Examples:

- UC ignores torture → York treats you as “not current student” → SOAS treats you as “incomplete applicant.”  
- FCDO labels you vexatious → UC treats your ADA packet as optional → Trinity treats your disability as inconvenience.  
- Courts deny legal personhood → universities treat your identity as unstable → accessibility offices treat your communication as suspect.

### **Harm Echo is a structural cascade.**

It creates:

- isolation,  
- procedural abandonment,  
- retraumatization,  
- and denial of remedy.

It is the opposite of Article 14 rehabilitation.

Institutions struggle with your dual status:

### **Duality:**  
You are simultaneously:
